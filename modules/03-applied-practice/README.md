| **Purpose** | Learners apply CRAFT and the morning's techniques to a messy, realistic case, and practise the two habits of responsible use: checking what they paste, and verifying what they get |

Slides 25–33 of the deck.

No new concepts. The two habits introduced at 13:07 turn limits 1 and 4 from
module 01 (it makes things up; it isn't a vault) into routines.

## What goes here

- **The case:** Prairie Point Logistics is starting a hybrid work pilot. Groups
  get three fictional documents: the draft policy, an email from a manager
  (which contains an employee's name, ID, and performance-plan status, as a
  deliberate privacy trap), and six town-hall questions, three of which the
  policy doesn't answer.
- **Deliverables:** a staff FAQ grounded in the policy, manager talking points
  that name no one, and a verification note.
- **Which account you're in (13:07).** With a work or school account covered
  by the organization's Gemini license, chats aren't used to train Google's
  models or reviewed by people. With a personal Google account, chats may be
  read by human reviewers and used to improve Google's products; reviewed chats
  are kept up to three years even after deletion. Source: Google's Gemini Apps
  Privacy Hub and the Workspace with Gemini FAQ. Re-check both the week before
  you teach.
- **Two habits:** the **data traffic light** (green: public or fictional, any
  account; yellow: internal, only in a licensed work or school account; red:
  personal, confidential, or regulated, never paste) and the **verify** check
  (accurate, complete, invented, fair, fit for purpose), including opening
  Gemini's **Sources** links when they appear.
- **Reference solution:**
  [materials/data/reference-solutions.md](../../materials/data/reference-solutions.md#activity-31-hybrid-pilot-case),
  not shown until 14:05.

## Activities

### Energizer (13:00)

Each table group gets a flipchart sheet with two headings, **CRAFT** and
**Techniques**. In five minutes they write everything they remember, without
notes. Then 2 minutes for everyone to look at the other sheets.

### Activity 3.1: Prairie Point hybrid pilot

- **Format:** groups of 3–4, 35 minutes, with roles: navigator (writes prompts),
  driver (types and runs them), checker (compares with the source and watches
  for personal information)
- **Task:** the three deliverables above. Full instructions are in the
  [workbook](../../materials/handouts/workbook.md#module-03).
- **Checkpoint (13:32):** each group says in one sentence where they are, and
  whether they've decided what to do with Dana's email. Hand out hint cards
  as needed.
- **Done looks like:** every FAQ answer traced to the policy or marked *To
  confirm with HR*; no personal details pasted; verification note complete
- **Extension:** leadership has just made Friday a core in-office day too.
  Update the FAQ in one follow-up prompt.

**Hint cards** (print and cut):

1. *The AI answered a question the policy doesn't cover.* → Add: "Use only the
   policy. If the policy doesn't answer a question, write *To confirm with
   HR*."
2. *Not sure what to do with Dana's email.* → Don't paste it. Describe the
   concern in general terms: "A manager asks whether performance can be a factor
   in approving remote work, and how to cover a front desk on remote days."
3. *The output is long and generic.* → Add the format and the audience, and
   give one example FAQ answer in the style you want.

### Gallery walk (13:50)

Groups leave their FAQ on screen and move clockwise. At each stop they leave
one sticky note: *one thing they did well; one answer we'd double-check.* After
three rotations, each group goes back and reads its notes.

## Facilitation notes

- Watch for groups pasting Dana's whole email. Don't stop them; ask the
  checker "what's in that email?" Groups that find it themselves remember it.
- The three unanswerable questions (2, 4's edge case, 6) are where the AI
  most often invents answers. Ask groups what the policy says, word for word.
- Write down good prompts as you circulate and use them, with permission, at
  14:05.

## If running late

Replace the gallery walk with two groups presenting for 4 minutes each (saves 7
minutes). Choose one group that caught an invented answer and one that handled
the privacy trap well.
